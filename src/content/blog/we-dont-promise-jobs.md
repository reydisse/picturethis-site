**Plenty of programs promise you a career. We won't make that promise, because it costs nothing to make, and everything to believe.**

Here's what we promise instead: hours on real gear at real shows, working to a professional standard. Honest instruction from working technicians, people who mixed, switched, or lit a show this month. A reference from people the industry knows. And a crew that'll say your name when a call comes in.

The first gigs our graduates go after are usually cable and stagehand calls. Then they move into operating roles: audio assist, camera, lighting op. That's how this industry really hires, through reputation and by seeing who shows up ready to work. We teach the craft and the professionalism, because both are part of the job.

Every participant is matched with a mentor, and mentoring here comes with clear rules. Every mentor passes a Vulnerable Sector Check and follows a written code of conduct. Mentors and participants meet in crew spaces, never alone off-site. Participants who are 16 or 17 need a parent or guardian's consent to take part. These aren't fine-print details. They're the program.

![At the control desk, where the standard is set](https://doop.design/photos/pexels/34623187.jpeg?h=650&w=940)
*Illustrative photography*

And the pathway doesn't stop at operating. Participants who work well on a crew learn production management: advancing the event, building the run sheet, managing the budget, and calling the show. Those who want to go further learn technical management: planning the rig, signing off on safety, and teaching the next cohort. The goal isn't just a job. It's a ladder, from your first cable to leading the room.

If that sounds like the way you want to learn, the next cohort starts in January. Twenty places, free, gear provided, ages 16 to 29.
